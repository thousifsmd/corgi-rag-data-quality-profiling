\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems are only as reliable as the corpus they
retrieve from, yet that corpus---documents, chunks, and embeddings---is almost never
profiled or quality-controlled the way a governed database is. Defects such as semantic
duplication, staleness, topical gaps, and cross-source contradiction are discovered only
reactively, through poor answers, after they have inflated token cost and hallucination.
We present \textbf{CORGI}, a system that (i)~profiles a RAG corpus with computable
data-quality metrics at the chunk and embedding level, (ii)~predicts which content will
degrade retrieval, and (iii)~gates or quarantines that content before indexing. On
\num{public IR/RAG benchmarks} and a heterogeneous energy-sector document lake
(SEC 10-K filings), CORGI \num{improves retrieval accuracy and groundedness by X\%/Y\%}
while \num{reducing corpus size by Z\% and per-query tokens by W\%} versus ungated and
naive-deduplication baselines; a controlled defect-injection study establishes a
\emph{causal} link from corpus-quality signals to downstream quality. We release the system
and a reusable RAG corpus-quality benchmark.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data-Quality Model for RAG Corpora}\label{sec:model}
Classical data-quality (DQ) theory defines dimensions---completeness, validity,
uniqueness, timeliness---over the rows and columns of structured data, where a cell is
either present or absent and two rows are either equal or not. A RAG corpus has no such
schema: its atoms are \emph{chunks} of free text and their vector \emph{embeddings}, for
which equality is approximate and ``missing'' is ill-defined. We therefore re-operationalize
each dimension as a computable function over chunks and embeddings. Every definition below is
realized exactly in our reference implementation, so the model is testable rather than
nominal.

\subsection{Notation}
A corpus $\mathcal{C}$ is a set of documents; each document $d$ carries a timestamp $t_d$ and
is segmented into \emph{sentence-aligned} chunks (whole sentences packed to a target of $W$
words without splitting a sentence; over-long table/header runs are hard-wrapped). Let
$\mathcal{K}=\{c_1,\dots,c_N\}$ be the resulting chunk set. Each chunk $c$ has a token length
$\lvert c\rvert$ and a unit embedding $\mathbf{e}(c)\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$,
$\lVert\mathbf{e}(c)\rVert_2=1$, so that cosine similarity reduces to the dot product
$\mathbf{e}(c)^{\!\top}\mathbf{e}(c')$.

\subsection{Dimensions}
\textbf{Completeness} penalizes degenerate chunks. With a token floor $\theta$, the
thin-chunk ratio is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Comp} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{K}} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\lvert c\rvert < \theta\right].
\end{equation}
At the document level we also report date completeness, the fraction of documents with a
usable $t_d$ (needed for timeliness).

\noindent\textbf{Validity} measures whether a chunk is a well-formed textual unit. A chunk is
boundary-valid if it begins with a capital, digit, or opening quotation mark and ends in
sentence-final punctuation; the defect rate is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Val} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{K}} \mathbf{1}\!\left[c \text{ is not boundary-valid}\right].
\end{equation}
Under sentence-aligned chunking this isolates genuinely malformed units (truncated tables,
headers), not the arbitrary cuts produced by fixed-width windows.

\noindent\textbf{Uniqueness} has two forms. The exact-duplicate ratio hashes a
whitespace/case-normalized chunk, $h(c)$, giving
$\mathrm{Uniq}_{\mathrm{ex}} = 1 - \lvert\{h(c)\}\rvert / N$. The \emph{semantic redundancy}
ratio is the fraction of chunks whose nearest \emph{other} chunk exceeds a similarity
threshold $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Red}_{\tau} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{K}}
\mathbf{1}\!\left[\, s(c) \ge \tau \,\right],\qquad
s(c)=\max_{c'\neq c}\ \mathbf{e}(c)^{\!\top}\mathbf{e}(c'),
\end{equation}
with $s(c)$ computed by approximate nearest-neighbor search to keep the pass near-linear
rather than $O(N^2)$.

\noindent\textbf{Timeliness} inherits each chunk's document date. With reference time
$t_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and age $a(c)=t_{\mathrm{ref}}-t_{d(c)}$ (years), the stale ratio for a
freshness horizon $\Delta$ is
$\mathrm{Stale}_{\Delta}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{c}\mathbf{1}[a(c)\ge\Delta]$; we also report mean age.

\noindent\textbf{Coverage} summarizes how evenly the corpus spans its topic space. We cluster
$\{\mathbf{e}(c)\}$ into $K$ groups and, with cluster proportions $p_1,\dots,p_K$, report the
normalized entropy
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Cov} \;=\; -\frac{1}{\ln K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} p_k \ln p_k \;\in\; [0,1],
\end{equation}
where $1$ is perfectly balanced coverage and low values indicate skew or gaps. Table~\ref{tab:dims}
summarizes the mapping.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Data-quality dimensions re-operationalized for a RAG corpus.}
\label{tab:dims}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Dimension & Signal & Metric \\
\midrule
Completeness & degenerate chunks & thin-chunk ratio $\mathrm{Comp}$ \\
Validity & chunk boundary quality & bad-boundary ratio $\mathrm{Val}$ \\
Uniqueness & exact + embedding near-dups & $\mathrm{Uniq}_{\mathrm{ex}}$, $\mathrm{Red}_{\tau}$ \\
Timeliness & chunk age vs.\ horizon & stale ratio $\mathrm{Stale}_{\Delta}$ \\

\end{tabular}

\subsection{Corpus profile (C1)}
Table~\ref{tab:profile} reports the measured profile of the EDGAR case-study corpus:
50 annual reports (10-K) from ten major energy issuers over fiscal years 2021--2025
(filed 2022--2026), segmented into 22{,}063 sentence-aligned chunks and embedded with a
384-dimensional sentence encoder. The dominant finding is redundancy: at $\tau=0.95$,
\textbf{57.1\%} of chunks have a near-duplicate, with a mean nearest-neighbor similarity of
$0.94$. Crucially, exact deduplication removes only $6.1\%$---so embedding-level profiling
surfaces roughly $9\times$ more redundant content than byte-identical matching, and about
$51$ further percentage points that exact methods cannot see. Timeliness and validity are
likewise material: $39.8\%$ of content is at least three years old, and $10.1\%$ of chunks
are malformed units (truncated tables and headers). This redundancy is not an artifact of the
similarity threshold (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}): even at a stringent $\tau=0.99$, $32.5\%$ of
chunks retain a near-duplicate, rising to $75.4\%$ at $\tau=0.90$.
